## Required host closure

1. Surviving Supervisor observes actual guard exit/loss through an event-driven owned-process channel, withdraws Ready, latches activation disabled and initiates stock restoration. A replacement guard is not a prerequisite to recovery; do not add recursive watchers. A missing lease may use a bounded monotonic deadline, but elapsed time cannot prove successful restoration.
2. Actual child-process tests cover guard exit after Ready and at activation boundaries, including the race between guard loss and committing injection configuration. No injection commit may proceed after protection is known lost. If activation already started, restore stock. Assert exact baseline/unrelated bytes and actual stock child identity before harness cleanup is allowed to run. Demonstrate injected child termination and no second activation. Independent review must inspect these assertions and the observer's separation from cleanup.
3. Exercise simultaneous restoration requests from surviving actors using one transaction identity and an idempotent restore operation. An owner lock must not strand recovery when its owner dies. Late writes from a canceled activation must not reinstall injection after restoration. Test partial writes, mismatched transaction ownership and stale process identities.
4. Blocked/failed restoration must remain RecoveryFailed with evidence retained and no new activation. A watchdog reports failure; it does not make a stuck filesystem or service-manager operation interruptible. Record which operations can remain outstanding and prevent late activation effects. Do not advertise a five-second recovery guarantee or use the outer 30-second harness termination as a stock-restoration receipt.

These close concrete host gaps; they do not simulate away service-manager, kernel-I/O or watchdog failure. If the target recovery path cannot meet the device margin below, reject E1 entry even after host tests pass.

### Prioritized stock-name launcher investigation

Candidate: one reviewed runtime-only ExecStart override replaces the command with an original small launcher that ultimately execs the unchanged stock executable/arguments under the same PID/service. An ExecStart list reset is distinct from an OnFailure dependency reset; verify effective commands in the dummy fixture. Preserve vendor Restart=on-failure and RestartMode=direct rather than silently changing failure policy. No unit-wide LD_PRELOAD or inherited loader environment: construct the injected environment only for the one intended xochitl exec. Launcher, guard and helpers must never load XOVI. Every fallback constructs the exact qualified stock environment.

The launcher checks exact manifest, boot/transaction generation and armed recovery, atomically consumes a single irreversible attempt claim **before** injected exec, and otherwise execs stock. Missing, spent, corrupt or ambiguous eligibility chooses stock, never another injection. No claim deletion on child exit/restart; no regeneration from Supervisor restart or a late request. If injected exec fails, the already-spent claim still forces stock fallback. The claim file alone does not close check-to-exec races: define serialization/ownership so restoration fences the launcher and accepted service jobs, or waits for its exact exec outcome and removes any injected generation before issuing a stock receipt. Pipe EOF is not proof its writer has exited. Receipt issuance requires actual process/job resolution, not a cached token.

This can avoid repeated injected starts while retaining service-name coupling, but it cannot promise that direct mode prevents every vendor failure action. Stock fallback/launcher failure, existing start-limit consumption, stop/abort failure or unresolved manager jobs can still reach the vendor handler. Do not reset global or unrelated failure counters to create artificial headroom. The exact packet needs bounded claim I/O, launcher liveness, failure-path/start-limit analysis, recovery when a claim writer blocks, and normal stock shutdown preservation. If those cannot be justified, reject this candidate rather than broadening handler overrides silently. Simulate the mechanism using harmless stand-ins first; E0T may measure manager behavior but never launches this wrapper around actual xochitl.

### Separate transient-service investigation

Investigate an exact unique transaction-named service, never an alias for xochitl.service. It would run the same qualified executable/arguments with a vetted loader through its own explicit environment, one attempt, Restart=no and no copied vendor OnFailure dependency. Confirm the **effective** transient unit has no inherited failure handler, including generic service drop-ins. Its independent recovery actor must be outside both runtime and Supervisor kill domains. Manager-global environment remains untouched. No production packaging selection follows.

Before starting it, an explicit normal stock stop must reach a confirmed terminal job outcome with the original cgroup/process gone. Preserve document state through normal shutdown; do not promote an outer timeout or forced kill into graceful stop. A stop failure may itself enter the vendor failure path, so unchanged stock policy is not automatically safe. If that boundary cannot be bounded without an unreviewed policy change, reject the candidate. Later stock start is issued only after the transient cgroup is empty, its start/stop jobs are resolved and its activation authority is irrevocably latched off. A queued StartTransientUnit request must not create a process after recovery observes an empty cgroup; track the accepted request/job and its final disposition, including lost replies. Do not reuse unit names or blindly retry unknown starts.

Investigate these coupling gates using read-only metadata/static evidence before device activation:

## Device timing and finite operator sequence

Derive device bounds from the actual profile; do not transplant E0's 5-second readiness/rollback values. Define readiness deadline A, maximum fault-detection delay D, restoration bound R (including stop, config restoration, manager reload and stock start), verification bound V, explicit uncertainty margin M and earliest relevant watchdog/reboot deadline W. For every relevant watchdog scope, show the conservative worst case fits its remaining budget; when one window covers the whole attempt, require A + D + R + V + M < W. Document the clock origin and reset semantics rather than assuming heartbeats reset every watchdog. Infinite/unknown service jobs or unknown W cannot be made safe by inventing a timeout; refuse until a bounded route is justified. A profile with no disruptive watchdog still needs finite reviewed bounds for every stage.
